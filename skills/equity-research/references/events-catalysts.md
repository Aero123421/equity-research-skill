liquidity. If magnitude matters, look at this stock's own past reactions.

## 1. Method: seven questions for any event

1. **What exactly happened** per the primary document (TDnet/EDINET filing,
   8-K, press release, court docket, regulator letter)? Read the filing, not
   the headline.
2. **Mechanical vs informational effect:** cash, share count, ownership,
   index weight, forced buyers/sellers — versus what the market now knows.
3. **What was already priced in?** Run-up, options-implied move, short
   interest / 信用残, rumor history, whether the event was scheduled and
   rule-based (index changes are front-run).
4. **Terms and probability:** price, premium, conditions, approvals,
   timeline, break price, who can block.
5. **Who else is affected?** Peers (read-through), suppliers/customers,
   counterparties (block seller, lender, parent/subsidiary, cross-holder),
   index and ETF holders, FX/rate-sensitive names.
6. **Which second event does it make likely?** Buyback → ToSTNeT-3 block;
   TOB → squeeze-out and delisting; spin-off → forced selling; equity raise →
   lock-up/overhang; delisting notice → reverse split → dilution; tariff
   ruling → substitute tariffs and one-off refund gains.
7. **What would prove the reading wrong, and when is the next dated
   checkpoint?**

Japanese disclosures often come as a bundle after the close (results +
forecast revision + dividend + buyback). Decompose the bundle before
attributing the price move.

## 2. Rule-change ledger (as of 2026-10; verify before relying)

These changes make older playbooks wrong. Re-check status when it matters.

**Japan**
- **TOB rules (2024 FIEA amendment), effective 2026-05-01:** mandatory-bid
  threshold lowered from one-third to **30%**; on-exchange purchases now
  covered; the "rapid acquisition" rule abolished; new de-minimis exception.
  Large-holding reporting clarified "joint holders" and "important proposal
  acts" to ease collaborative engagement.
- **Listing maintenance:** Prime/Standard/Growth transition measures ended
  (full criteria for record dates from 2025-03-01). Growth market: market
  cap ¥10bn after 5 years of listing, applying from fiscal years ending on or
  after 2030-03-01 (as announced; check final rules).
- **TOPIX second-stage reform:** from October 2026, constituents across all
  segments are selected by float market cap and liquidity; names that fail
  have their weight reduced in eight quarterly steps (≈2 years), with a
  re-evaluation in October 2027. A slow mechanical headwind for affected
  names, not a single cliff.
- **Corporate Governance Code revision** finalized July 2026 (pre-AGM annual
  report disclosure, resource allocation for growth); check effective date.
- **Disclosure:** 四半期報告書 abolished from April 2024; quarterly 決算短信
  continues. 特設注意市場銘柄 is now 特別注意銘柄 (one-year remediation cap).

**US**
- **13D/13G deadlines shortened** (13D within 5 business days; amendments
  within 2 business days).
- **HSR:** the expanded 2025 premerger form was vacated by a court in
  February 2026 (appeal pending); thresholds are adjusted annually.
- **SEC semiannual reporting:** an optional semiannual report (instead of
  10-Q) was proposed in May 2026; as of October 2026 no adoption was found —
  check whether a company you study has switched.
- **Short-position reporting (Form SHO / Rule 13f-2):** compliance delayed to
  January 2028; no aggregated public data yet. FINRA short interest remains
  twice monthly.
- **Russell US indexes reconstitute semiannually from 2026** (June and
  December). Nasdaq-100 added a "Fast Entry" rule for very large new listings
  (2026). S&P 500 kept its 12-month seasoning and GAAP-profitability rules.
- **Nasdaq $1 bid rule:** non-compliance capped at 360 days; a reverse split
  in the prior year removes the compliance period.
- **Tariffs:** IEEPA tariffs were struck down in February 2026 and replaced
  by tariffs under other authorities; refunds are not automatic. Look up the
  current legal basis and rates.

## 3. Japan events

### TOB (公開買付), MBO, take-private
Check:
- Offer price and premium vs 1/3/6-month VWAP and last close; vs book value
  (PBR) and any activist's stated fair value; vs the independent valuation
  (DCF range) in the filing.
- Target's stance in 意見表明報告書 (賛同/反対/留保); special committee,
  fairness opinion, independence of the process.
- Conditions: 買付予定数の下限/上限, majority-of-minority, regulatory
  approvals (FEFTA, foreign antitrust), financing, tender period and
  extensions.
- Tender agreements (応募契約): committed holders + acquirer vs the minimum.
- Second step: squeeze-out (株式併合 etc.), usually at the same price;
  appraisal rights. Delisting timeline (整理銘柄).
- Since 2026-05-01 the trigger is 30% and includes market purchases.
Reading: trading above the offer = market expects a bump or competing bid.
Partial offers leave stubs; a missed minimum can sink a board-supported deal.
Also investigate: other low-PBR / cash-rich / parent-subsidiary names in the
sector; activists and cross-holders in the register; acquirer financing and
rating; the target's listed subsidiaries; possible rival bidders.

### Parent-subsidiary unwinding (親子上場解消, 完全子会社化)
Check: cash price or exchange ratio vs market-cap ratio, minority protection,
parent rationale and financing, size of the subsidiary within the parent.
Also investigate: other listed subsidiaries of the same parent and of other
groups ("next candidate"), the parent's capital allocation.

### Unsolicited / hostile bids (同意なき買収)
METI's 2023 takeover guidelines expect boards to seriously consider bona fide
proposals via special committees; defenses must be proportionate and rest on
shareholder intent. Check board response and timeline, defense measures,
shareholder vote dates, bidder financing and plans. A board rejection is
rarely the end.

### Activists and shareholder proposals
June AGM season for March-FY companies. Check proponent and track record,
proposal type (buybacks, DOE, directors, cross-holdings), board's opposition,
proxy advisers, voting results (臨時報告書 after the AGM), and the share
register (cross-holders/friendly holders decide outcomes). Concessions before
the AGM and support percentage matter more than pass/fail. Also investigate:
the activist's other Japanese positions (大量保有報告), peers with similar
balance sheets.

### Cross-shareholding sales (政策保有株式の売却)
Check seller, method (market, 売出し, issuer's ToSTNeT-3 buyback), % of
float, whether the issuer absorbs it with a buyback; the seller's one-off
gain (特別利益) is not run-rate earnings. Also investigate other names held
by the same seller (banks, insurers, trading houses).

### Buybacks (自社株買い)
Mechanics: the board sets a maximum (取得枠: shares and amount) and period;
actual purchases can fall well short. Methods: open market, ToSTNeT-3
(off-auction, at the previous close, usually a specific seller), self-tender.
Cancellation (消却) permanently removes shares; treasury shares can be
reissued.
Check: 枠 as % of shares outstanding and vs average daily trading value;
method; past execution rate (monthly 自己株券買付状況報告書); funding; whether
it offsets dilution; who is selling into it.
Misreadings: 枠 = spend; a buyback from a parent/cross-holder shrinking float
(it may not); ignoring CB/warrant dilution.

### Dividends, DOE, progressive dividends, record dates
権利付最終日 is two business days before the record date (T+2); the price
typically drops by about the dividend on 権利落ち日. Check payout vs FCF,
DOE floor, special/commemorative dividends (記念配当), split adjustments,
total shareholder return policy. Policy upgrades (DOE, 累進配当) can re-rate
low-PBR stocks.

### Index events (Japan)
- **TOPIX** (second-stage reform, see ledger): check the stock's float
  market-cap rank and turnover ratio; estimate passive flow (index-tracking
  assets × weight change) vs average daily trading value.
- **Nikkei 225:** regular reviews in April and October (announced early
  March / early September); price-weighted, so high-priced stocks and splits
  (via conversion factors) matter.
- **JPX-Nikkei 400** (annual review), **MSCI** (quarterly reviews:
  Feb/May/Aug/Nov announcements, effective at month-end), **FTSE**.
Misreadings: announced vs effective dates; inclusion ≠ permanent re-rating;
forgetting the opposite flow in deleted names.

### Equity financing (公募増資, 第三者割当, MSワラント, CB, 売出し, 立会外分売)
Check: use of proceeds (growth vs debt repayment vs rescue), dilution %,
pricing discount, who buys (strategic vs financial), pricing period. For MS
warrants (行使価額修正条項付新株予約権): reset mechanism, floor price, total
potential shares; the holder sells as it exercises (persistent overhang).
Large third-party allotments (e.g. dilution ≥25% or change of controlling
shareholder) need an independent opinion or shareholder approval under TSE
rules (verify current thresholds). Check going-concern notes and 債務超過.
売出し/立会外分売: who sells, % of float, overallotment/syndicate cover.
A credible strategic investor can de-risk; a "growth" label on an MS-warrant
deal can be rescue financing.

### Stock splits and shareholder benefits (株式分割, 株主優待)
Splits lower the minimum investment (100-share unit) and attract retail/NISA
flows; no fundamental change. 優待 abolition hits retail-heavy names; new
benefits give a bounce that fades without fundamentals. Check retail share of
register and 信用残; record-date effects and 信用クロス.

### Listing criteria, supervision and delisting (上場維持基準, 監理/整理銘柄)
Check which criterion binds (float market cap, float ratio, trading value,
shareholders) and how far the company is from it; the improvement period;
fixes (売出し, 立会外分売, splits, equity raise, segment transfer, or going
private). Other delisting grounds: 債務超過, delayed annual report, false
statements, internal-control failures (特別注意銘柄).

### Earnings season and forecast revisions (決算, 業績予想の修正)
March-FY calendar: full-year results in May; Q1 late Jul–mid Aug; Q2/H1 late
Oct–mid Nov; Q3 late Jan–mid Feb. TSE requires prompt disclosure when a new
forecast differs from the last by more than set thresholds (commonly cited:
sales ±10%, profits ±30%; verify in the 適時開示 guidebook). Many companies
guide conservatively at the start of the year — check the company's history
of initial forecast vs actual before calling an upward revision a surprise.
Compare with consensus, check the FX assumption vs spot, decompose one-offs.
After-close disclosures move PTS and the next open.

### Scandals (不祥事, 第三者委員会)
Process: disclosure → third-party committee → possible delay of filings →
restatement → improvement report → possible 特別注意銘柄 designation. Check
restatement size, audit opinion, management involvement, regulators,
covenants, customers' reaction, recurrence risk. The third-party report is
often not the bottom. Also investigate the auditor's other clients and peers
with the same practice (industry-wide issues).

### Derivatives expiries (SQ)
Nikkei 225 futures/options major SQ on the second Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec;
monthly options SQ other months. Can cause volatility in index-heavy names.

## 4. US events

### Earnings, pre-announcements, guidance, investor days
Check: revenue/EPS vs consensus (and any whisper), guidance range midpoint vs
consensus (next quarter and full year), quality of the beat (tax, buybacks,
FX, one-offs, working capital), segment mix, backlog/RPO, cash conversion,
options-implied move vs history, positioning. Guidance usually matters more
than the quarter. Investor-day targets set the multi-year bar. Also
investigate peers and suppliers reporting soon (including Asian suppliers'
monthly data) and customers' capex commentary.

### M&A and merger arbitrage
Check: consideration (cash/stock, collar, fixed/floating ratio), financing,
MAC, termination and reverse-termination fees, go-shop, votes, regulatory
approvals (HSR, international antitrust, CFIUS for foreign buyers), outside
date. Spread math: gross spread = (deal value − price)/price, annualize to
expected close; implied probability ≈ (P − downside)/(deal value −
downside). A wide spread points to a specific risk — find it. Acquirers in
stock deals are often shorted by arbs. Also investigate peers ("who's
next"), both parties' suppliers/customers, lenders, rival bidders.

### Spin-offs
Check Form 10, debt allocation, transition services agreements, stranded
costs, management incentives, index treatment. Expect forced selling of the
spinco by holders for whom it doesn't fit; value each piece with leverage
and dis-synergies.

### Activism and proxy fights
13D within 5 business days of crossing 5%. Check activist track record and
thesis, stake and derivatives, company response, nomination window and
meeting date, proxy advisers. The stock may have run before the 13D. Also
investigate the activist's other holdings and copycat targets.

### Buybacks, dividends, splits
Authorization ≠ execution: check 10-Q/10-K repurchase tables, ASR vs open
market, net of SBC dilution, funding source, 1% excise tax on net
repurchases. Dividend cuts hurt more than initiations help. Splits change
no fundamentals. US ex-dividend date = record date under T+1.

### Secondary offerings, ATMs, convertibles, shelf registrations
Check S-3/424B filings, ATM size vs float and ADV, discount, use of proceeds,
insider selling, convertible terms and hedging (arbs short the stock),
capped calls, going-concern language, cash runway.

### Lock-up expiries and IPO quiet period
Check % of shares unlocking vs float and ADV, holder types and cost basis,
early-release triggers and waivers, analyst initiation dates after the quiet
period, index fast-entry/seasoning dates.

### Index events (S&P 500, Russell, Nasdaq-100) and expirations
- **S&P 500:** committee-based; US domicile, market-cap minimum (reviewed
  periodically), float, liquidity, 12-month seasoning, positive GAAP earnings
  (latest quarter and trailing four quarters). Quarterly rebalance effective
  after the third Friday of Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec; ad hoc changes for M&A.
- **Russell:** semiannual from 2026 (June and December; rank day, preliminary
  lists, effective date). Moves between Russell 1000 and 2000 shift demand.
- **Nasdaq-100:** annual December reconstitution, quarterly rebalances, Fast
  Entry for very large new listings.
- **OPEX / triple witching:** third Friday monthly; quarterly coincides with
  index rebalances; pinning near large open-interest strikes.
Misreadings: adjusted profit ≠ GAAP profit for S&P eligibility; the deleted
names get the opposite flow; mechanical price pressure often reverses.

### FDA, clinical trials, regulatory approvals
Check PDUFA goal date and application type, advisory committee, label,
manufacturing inspection history (including contract manufacturers), prior
CRLs, trial design and endpoints, cash runway, partners and milestones,
competitor timelines. A PDUFA date is not a guaranteed decision date; a
positive AdCom is not approval. Also investigate CDMOs (including Japanese),
licensors/royalty holders, competitors in the class.

### Litigation, antitrust and regulatory rulings
Check procedural stage, liability vs remedy, damages vs market cap,
insurance, appeals timeline. The surprise is usually the remedy relative to
what was feared. Also investigate customers, competitors and complementors
affected by remedies, and parallel cases in other jurisdictions.

### Short-seller reports
Check whether allegations are specific and verifiable against filings,
the short seller's incentives, company and audit-committee response,
auditor actions, short interest, borrow cost and options skew. Judge the
facts, not the author's reputation. Also investigate named customers,
suppliers, auditor and companies with similar structures.

### Credit events, delisting notices, bankruptcy
Check maturity wall, covenants, liquidity, rating thresholds (IG/HY
boundary → forced selling), delisting notices (Nasdaq $1 rule), reverse
splits followed by dilutive raises. In Chapter 11, existing equity is usually
cancelled. Also investigate suppliers with receivables (including Japanese
companies), landlords, competitors gaining share.

### Tariffs, export controls, sanctions
Check the legal basis (durability and litigation risk), product scope,
exemptions, effective dates, pass-through and inventory, supply-chain
rerouting, FX offsets, refund receivables (one-off gains). Track dated
expiries of suspensions and reviews. Also investigate Japanese exporters
with US exposure, semicap makers exposed to China controls, materials
dependent on restricted minerals, logistics.

## 5. News, analyst actions and sentiment (L12)

Minimum check:
- Material news in the last 1–3 months with dates; distinguish company
  disclosures from media reports and rumors.
- Analyst rating and target-price changes and estimate revision direction
  (if findable). A string of downward estimate revisions often matters more
  than a single rating change.
- Notable reports from short sellers or activists.
- For Japanese stocks, check also 四季報 forecast changes vs company forecast
  where available, and retail interest (message boards, 信用買い残 build-up)
  as a crowding signal.

Interpretation:
- Narrative crowding: when every article tells the same story, check
  positioning and expectations — the story may be fully priced.
- Media "関連銘柄" lists are not evidence of exposure (see
  `related-companies.md`).
- Separate "news caused the move" from "news explained after the move".
  Check timestamps against intraday price action when possible.
- Analyst price targets are not market expectations; consensus EPS is closer
  to what is priced.
- Treat anonymous social-media claims as unverified leads only.

## 6. Building a catalyst calendar

For the user's horizon (default: next 3 months), list dated items:

- Company: earnings date, AGM, investor day, dividend record dates, buyback
  period end, lock-up expiry, deal milestones (tender period end, votes,
  regulatory deadlines), product launches, PDUFA dates.
- Related companies: peers', key customers' and suppliers' earnings dates;
  monthly data releases (e.g. TSMC revenue, 月次売上).
- Market structure: index reviews (TOPIX, Nikkei 225, MSCI, S&P, Russell,
  Nasdaq-100), SQ/OPEX, 13F deadlines, short interest release dates, margin
  data release days.
- Macro: central bank meetings, CPI, jobs, GDP, Tankan, policy deadlines
  (tariff and export-control expiries).

Mark each as confirmed (with source) or estimated.
